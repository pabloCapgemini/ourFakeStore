# Slide 3 — Modern Software Engineering: Review Comments

## What's still accurate
- The overall timeline structure (IDP → coding assistants → agentic) remains valid and well-sequenced
- The 2017 and 2021 entries hold up — no substantive changes needed
- The observation about inexperienced developers becoming dependent on AI (2021) is even more validated

## What's outdated / needs updating

### 2025 Q2 entry — extend, don't replace
- "AI Agents and Agentic Software Engineering" is now well established — the framing should move from *emergence* to *maturation*
- The entry should be extended to reflect what became clear in H2 2025: **agent-to-agent workflows** are a distinct and growing pattern, not just single-agent delegation
- Multi-agent orchestration (one agent planning, others executing, others reviewing) is now a practical reality teams are navigating

## Proposed update to 2025 entry
> **AI Agents, Agentic Software Engineering & Agent-to-Agent Workflows** *(2025 Q2 onwards)*
>
> Team composition critical: expertise in software engineering is essential to craft prompts and processes and to understand what AI is producing. As agent-to-agent workflows emerge, **core software engineering principles — modularity, separation of concerns, testability, clear interfaces — become the foundation that makes agentic systems sustainable**. Without them, complexity compounds rapidly across agent boundaries and systems become unmaintainable.

## Key emphasis to add
- **Over-emphasise** this point: the teams that succeed with agentic workflows are the ones with the strongest software engineering fundamentals — not the ones with the most AI tooling
- The irony: AI can accelerate delivery dramatically, but only if the underlying engineering discipline is already strong enough to contain and direct it
- This is a counter-narrative to "anyone can vibe-code now" — **rigour matters more, not less**
